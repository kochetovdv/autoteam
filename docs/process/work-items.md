# Work items and review history

A work item owns one outcome and the evidence that it is accepted. Keep the existing `P-*` package identifier: a package is the work item, not a second object to synchronize. Extend its established record in place. If none exists, use a record such as `docs/process/work-items/P-01.md` in the product, based on [the task template](../../templates/task.md). Plans, boards, cycle state and indexes link to this record; they are views, never another editable task registry.

Communication, authorization and autonomous continuation follow [orchestration](orchestration.md). This document defines the durable record and its events. Use [project knowledge](project-knowledge.md) for artifact ownership and discovery, [assurance](assurance.md) for assurance beyond one delivery, and [resource lifecycle](resource-lifecycle.md) for shared resources and cleanup.

## Enough preparation for the risk

Choose the route by uncertainty and consequence, not change size alone. A short route may keep the brief, plan, acceptance criteria and review history in the same P-record. A full route links a versioned specification, contracts, architecture decisions and plan where boundaries, data, security or other costly behavior change. R&D records the hypothesis, baseline, experiment, decision rule and resulting scope before promising an implementation. Do not require a separate large document or a fixed number of subtasks for a minor change.

For complex changes to existing code, first inspect the relevant entry points, behavior, data flow, contracts, tests and operational constraints. Record what was inspected and what remains unknown. Use this reconnaissance to formulate the specification; do not infer intended behavior from code alone. A read-only investigation saves its artifacts in an allowed output workspace and links them. It does not initialize, copy or commit the repository under investigation just to keep process records.

Before implementation, reconcile requirements, plan and work items. Record the exact revisions compared, the REQ/NFR and acceptance criteria covered by each P-item, missing or conflicting obligations, dependencies and the resolution. Lightweight work may do this inline. Do not start a dependent implementation on a known contradiction. A later behavior decision amends the specification, plan and affected P-items together; a specification is maintained with behavior, not frozen after its first approval.

## Authoritative record

The record contains only the detail needed to execute and judge its outcome:

- Outcome, business or user impact, in/out boundaries and observable acceptance criteria. Preserve the goal anchor: the original problem and primary evidence or authorized decision that establish the intended outcome. Each criterion names how failure differs from success; map to REQ/NFR where they exist. For consequential or disputed meaning, link an independent derivation of the critical expected result from those sources, not only from the derived specification; follow [assurance](assurance.md).
- Inputs pinned to an immutable version or content hash: assignment/brief, requirements, plan, contracts, relevant knowledge and source. A branch name or `HEAD` alone is insufficient. For a dirty tree, include base commit plus the hash and location of a recoverable patch/snapshot, including relevant untracked inputs; a commit hash does not describe uncommitted work.
- Dependencies, writable boundaries, executor and reviewer assignments, checks, risk and handoff conditions. Actor IDs identify stable participants and run IDs identify executions; role, model and family are metadata, not identity. Record every contributing author for the candidate revision, including a reviewer or orchestrator who edited it.
- Current status and append-only events with time, actor/run, reason and evidence. Link candidate versions, check evidence, review records, findings, applicability decisions and the next responsible action. Chat messages are notification, not the record.

Evidence identifies the candidate/input versions, environment and relevant tool or dependency versions, command or procedure, result and exit status where available, covered criteria and limits. Link large output. State untested areas explicitly. A green check, a merged PR, silence or an agent's completion message alone cannot establish acceptance.

## Status follows events

An unprepared draft is not schedulable. Only the record owns these execution statuses; a blocked cause such as a resource is metadata, not a parallel status system.

| Status | Event that enters it | What must be recorded |
|---|---|---|
| `ready` | Preparation/reconciliation is complete and the next action has its inputs | accepted scope for the mode, pinned inputs, criteria, dependencies, executor and review route |
| `in-work` | Assigned executor claims a ready item, or receives a review return | actor/run, candidate baseline, writable boundary and next action; a return links findings |
| `on-review` | Executor submits a candidate and the automatic review handoff succeeds | candidate identity, authors, evidence, coverage/limits and independent reviewer assignments |
| `blocked` | A required input, decision, resource or dependency prevents the next action | prior status, affected action, blocker owner, request/resource link, next action and recheck trigger/date |
| `done` | Required independent acceptance applies to the delivered candidate and completion obligations are satisfied | current verdicts, findings dispositions, criterion coverage, delivered artifact and any follow-up owner |
| `cancelled` | The authorized owner withdraws an undelivered outcome | authority, reason/time, affected assignments and dependents, safe stop/settlement and cleanup owners |
| `superseded` | The authorized owner replaces an undelivered outcome with a different work item | cancellation details plus the successor link and explicit transfer of useful inputs/artifacts |

On a block, preserve the executor/reviewer assignment and previous status. Other independent work continues. Resolution produces an event with new evidence and returns to `ready`, `in-work` or `on-review` according to the actual next action; it does not silently jump to `done`. `CHANGES_REQUIRED` returns `on-review` to `in-work`; a missing acceptance input may instead block it. Record repeated returns against the goal anchor and the evidence of progress; patch count and model replacement are not progress. Apply the bounded critique and stalled-work protocol in [assurance](assurance.md). A changed accepted candidate or material input reopens `done` to the appropriate working status and makes affected acceptance stale until assessed. Corrections to a mistaken status are explicit events, never rewritten history.

`cancelled` and `superseded` are terminal non-delivery outcomes, not acceptance or delivered value. Stop new dispatch and invalidate the affected assignment generations. Request a safe stop of in-flight work, reconcile already-started effects and preserve late results as evidence; a late completion cannot reopen the item, trigger acceptance or authorize another effect. Do not assume cancelling a parent cancels every child: identify owned children and their stop/continue disposition under the existing authority; a shared child may still serve another outcome. Reconcile each dependent: replace its dependency explicitly, narrow its scope by an authorized decision, or block the affected action. Neither cancellation nor a successor link satisfies a delivery prerequisite or transfers acceptance automatically. A later renewed request becomes a linked new item. Previously delivered work retains its historical acceptance; a replacement does not relabel it as undelivered.

Count WIP per executor and per reviewer, including assigned blocked work. A terminal non-delivery item leaves the delivery queue, but unsettled runs, effects and cleanup remain visible obligations and consume their actual capacity until settled. Adjust assignments or capacity when queues grow; do not hide stalled items by marking them ready/done or repeatedly spawning replacements. Team size follows independent work, risk and resource capacity; no universal agent cap or checklist count is implied. Optional budgets aggregate parent, children, reviews, retries and cleanup according to [model routing](model-routing.md).

## Automatic, artifact-based handoff

The orchestrator tracks asynchronous task completion and consumes its linked artifacts. A handoff supplies the P-record, pinned inputs, candidate, evidence, boundaries and return format; no human copies transcripts between roles. Submission and acceptance are different events: task completion triggers the assigned review, a return routes findings to the responsible author, and a revised candidate triggers re-review. Record failures or waits and their owner; do not rely on a human nudge to advance an otherwise authorized step.

Persist the assignment identity/generation, expected artifact location and next owner before dispatch; persist a completion/return with its candidate and evidence before sending its wakeup. A reassignment receives a new generation; never reuse an invalidated one. Receivers reconcile the durable record against the current assignment and candidate, deduplicate replayed events, and record the next action before acknowledging the handoff. A timeout or missing wakeup is not proof of failure or permission to launch a replacement. Reconcile current runs, artifacts and recorded events first; late events from an invalidated assignment are retained but cannot advance execution.

Before relying on unattended handoff, identify a recovery owner, an actual independent wakeup/reconciliation mechanism and a maximum detection delay proportionate to the task. The mechanism must still run when the coordinator dies or its completion message is lost; its own in-memory timer and “check on restart” are insufficient. Reconciliation claims coordination through the ownership/lock protocol, then resumes only authorized pending actions without duplicating completed effects. Record the tested adapter or provider capability. Where none is available, disclose a manual-resume route and its continuity limit; a written rule is not automatic recovery and does not authorize installing a scheduler. Keep these fields in the existing work item or shared cycle recovery record, not a second task ledger.

An author cannot accept the authored candidate. A reviewer who changes its code, tests or other judged artifact becomes an author of the new candidate and needs another independent acceptor. Reassign explicitly and retain the contribution history. A different role label, model or run does not erase authorship.

Authorship independence does not make a shared interpretation correct. Critical expected behavior needs a source-based meaning check as well as candidate review; record who established it, from which primary evidence, and unresolved contradictions. A shared specification or its generated tests cannot by themselves supply that independence.

Use separate first verdicts for independent review lenses. Withhold peer verdicts and their conclusions until each reviewer has submitted an immutable first verdict; otherwise the second verdict is influenced confirmation. A reviewer may know the original assignment and shared factual inputs. The familiar independent reviewer normally rechecks fixes to preserve context and stable finding IDs. Add a fresh independent perspective when risk, disputed evidence, major revision or repeated returns warrants it; choose it explicitly rather than replacing context automatically. Model/family selection and limitations follow [model routing](model-routing.md).

## Review records, findings and applicability

Every review is an immutable record, embedded append-only in the P-record or linked using [the review template](../../templates/review.md). Record `RV-*`, the exact candidate and input/evidence versions, reviewer actor/run/model/family, independence, scope, findings and the verdict with reasons. First verdicts are preserved even after peer verdicts are revealed. A correction or re-review is a new record referring to the earlier one.

Findings retain stable IDs, for example `RV-01/F-01`. Fixes and rechecks reference that ID; do not renumber or delete inconvenient findings. Record evidence and a reason for each disposition: fixed, disproved, deferred or accepted exception by the authorized owner. A new finding gets a new ID. A contested finding returns to the familiar reviewer with the response evidence; unresolved disagreement follows orchestration's escalation and decision rules.

The original review never changes to pretend it reviewed a later candidate. The P-record records applicability to its current candidate as `current` or `stale`. A change to source, requirements, plan, contracts, relevant environment or evidence makes affected verdicts stale by default. Restore applicability only through a new review or an explicit carry-forward record: old/new versions and hashes, impact analysis of changed and dependent surfaces, criteria/findings still covered, evidence checked, limits, rationale and independent acceptor actor/run. The author cannot declare their own acceptance current. Unaffected verdicts may be carried forward with this justification; a shared label or green rerun is not justification by itself.

Completion requires current independent acceptance for the scoped outcome, resolved blocking findings, criteria satisfied with evidence and required documentation/handoff. An authorized exception states the residual impact, owner and follow-up; it does not relabel an unmet criterion as tested. Assurance or monitoring after delivery remains linked to the originating P-item and does not rewrite its review history.
