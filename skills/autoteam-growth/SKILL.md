# Growth

Take the product from "it exists" to "users come, stay, and pay". A channel is a hypothesis with a metric, not a belief.

**Input.** Positioning, ICP, product, public URLs, metrics (`autoteam-analytics`). **Output.** Prioritized channel map, page fixes, lifecycle messages, growth experiments with thresholds. **Forbidden.** Spam and purchased lists; dark patterns (hidden unsubscribe, fake scarcity, cancellation traps); inventing social proof; blocking AI bots without the human's decision. **Evidence.** A channel — CAC and conversion to an order of magnitude or ASSUMP; a page fix — before/after metric. **Stop.** None. Paid budget and large integrations — the human. Internal tool with no public URLs — N/A with a reason. **Next.** Landing page/screen → `autoteam-ui`. Content production → `autoteam-content`. Code (forms, emails, referral) → `autoteam-delivery`. Measurement → `autoteam-analytics`. Channel experiment → `autoteam-research`. Paid-spend limits → `autoteam-budget`.

## Funnel — end-to-end ownership

Touch → visit → signup → activation (first value) → habit → payment → recommendation. Fix the weakest link first: pouring traffic into leaky activation is burning money.

## Channels

From the ICP: where the segment already looks for a solution. 1–2 channels done deep beat five touched lightly. Each channel: hypothesis → small test → threshold (`autoteam-research`) → scale or drop. Media buying as craft is outside the canon (`docs/process/gaps.md`); channel design and evaluation live here.

## SEO and GEO

SEO — a list of links and a click; GEO — the model cites you in its answer. Common ground: one question per URL, the point in the first paragraph, speed, HTML in the index not only from JS, facts with sources. SEO: primary query, title/description. GEO: the answer right under an H2 question, a self-contained paragraph, FAQ/tables, `llms.txt` if requested. Don't confuse GEO with "geo" on a map — cover local search in a separate block.

## Activation and retention

Activation: steps to first value — fewer and faster; empty states lead by the hand. Retention: a reason to come back (habit, accumulated data, network); measure churn by cohorts, ask those who leave. Lifecycle emails/messages: event-driven (got stuck, came back, hit a plan limit), not "a digest for digest's sake".

## Referral

Only when retention is alive: a happy user + a moment of delight + a clear benefit for both sides. Referral on top of leaky retention is waste.
